A

The curriculum

Objectives · prerequisites · evidence

A scope and a sequence is not a curriculum

docs/03 gives what to teach and in what order. A curriculum is four things, and the fourth is the one that matters commercially: evidence, separated from teaching.

Today a card teaches a thing and then immediately asks about the thing. A child who answers correctly has demonstrated that they were awake ninety seconds ago. That is not evidence, it cannot be reported, and it is exactly why there is nothing honest to tell a parent.

Six strands, seventy-two objectives

docs/03's M/C/D strands describe the anatomy of a rung, not the curriculum's subject divisions. Those are:

StrandCoversWhy it stands alone
EARNWhere money comes from; work, value, time-for-money, the skill premium, income that isn't wagesThe only strand a child can act on from day one
CHOOSEScarcity, trade-off, opportunity cost, want vs need, unit price, advertising and dark patternsTransfers to real life fastest
KEEPSaving, allocation, goals, the buffer — and why a buffer exists before it is neededWhere habit beats knowledge
GROWInterest, compounding, risk, diversification, time horizon, feesNeeds the most maths; arrives latest
OWECredit as a priced tool, the cost of borrowing, repayment, arrears without shameMust be taught before it is met, not after
GUARDScams, phishing, purchase manipulation, gambling mechanics, privacyThe shortest path to real harm
6 strands× 12 rungs = 72 objectives in year one≈ 1.5 a week; the rest of the year is retrieval, doing and consequence

The objective record

Every objective is a data object, not a paragraph. This schema is the deliverable — without it there is nothing to assess against and nothing to report.

{
  "id": "CHOOSE-4",
  "strand": "CHOOSE",
  "rung": 4,
  "objective": "Given two sizes of the same thing at different prices, works out
                 which is cheaper per unit and can say why the bigger box is
                 not always better value.",
  "needs_maths": ["M5", "M8"],      // rungs of the number spine
  "needs": ["CHOOSE-2"],           // prerequisite objectives
  "surface": "shop",               // where in the town it is PRACTISED
  "teach": "c4b",
  "assess": ["a-CHOOSE-4-a", "a-CHOOSE-4-b", "a-CHOOSE-4-c"],
  "transfer": ["market-stall", "exchange-fee", "subscription"],
  "parent_line": "Ask her which is better value at the shop this week — and
                    let her be wrong once, because that is where it sticks.",
  "sources": []                    // non-empty for any real-world claim
}
  • An objective is written as a behaviour or it is not written. "Understands opportunity cost" is not an objective — you cannot watch it, so you cannot assess it and you must not report it. "Names what she gave up when she chose the kite over the kit" is one.
  • surface is mandatory. CLAUDE.md's existing rule — nothing is taught without a place in the town — promoted from a convention to a schema constraint that fails a build.

Three kinds of question. Only two are evidence.

KindWhenCounts as
CheckImmediately after the card, as todayAttention. Never reported, never scored.
Retrieval1 · 3 · 7 · 21 · 60 days later, different questionRetention
TransferApplied unprompted on a surface it wasn't taught onUnderstanding
The highest-value engineering task in this document

Transfer is worth the most and comes free from a simulation and from nothing else. A child taught unit price at the shop who then, unprompted, compares two brokerage fees at the Exchange has told you something no quiz can. Instrument the surfaces to record it.

Mastery states

unmet→ introduced→ practised→ retained→ transferred ·lapsed

Lapsed must be reportable and must not be hidden. A parent report that only ever goes up is a marketing document, and parents can smell one.

Mapping to something a parent or a school recognises

A curriculum nobody recognises is a private hobby. Candidates, in rough order of value to this audience:

FrameworkWhy it matters here
NCFE / NFLAT IndiaRBI-backed and school-facing — the single most valuable mapping for the primary and diaspora market
CFPB building-blocks youth model USDevelopmental rather than topical. Its claim that executive function and habits matter more than knowledge at these ages is the intellectual basis for this whole app.

Research task, not a completed mapping

Every one of these must be read and verified before a single objective claims to map to it, and long before any of it appears in a marketing claim. CONCEPT §6.6 — never teach a number from memory — applies to a standards citation exactly as it applies to an interest rate.

Placement, not a test

  • Framed as "let's find out where to start", never as a score.
  • Adaptive: about eight items, stopping as soon as a level is bracketed.
  • Assesses the number spine only — maths is the thing that genuinely blocks a surface. Concepts start at rung one for everyone; nobody is told they are behind on money at eight years old.
  • Re-run silently each season, so a child who has grown at school isn't held down by a placement taken a year ago.
  • Its output is a starting rung and a set of open surfaces. Never shown as a number, and never in the parent report as a score.
B

A year of content

182 hours · and how not to author them

The honest arithmetic

Thirty minutes × 365 days is 182 hours. Nobody authors 182 hours of children's content, and any plan that implies it is a plan to fail in month three.

So the requirement is not "write more cards". It is: authored content is the seed; the simulation generates the volume. The authored corpus for year one is finite and costable.

Year one · authored items
ItemCountKind
Teaching cards — one per objective72authored
Assessment items — 3 contexts × objective216authored
Townsfolk request templates40authored, parameterised
Season arc beats — 4 × ~1040authored
Family conversation cards — one a week52authored
World events — weather, tide, shock, festival60authored, parameterised
Total≈ 480a real but finite job

Everything else — market days, job rolls, quest instances, request parameters, price shocks, game difficulty variants — is generated from the child's own economy, which is why it never runs out and why it is always the right size for them.

The five engines that make the volume

  1. The market. Deterministic replay of real historical behaviour under fictional names, seeded per child. 365 market days a year, never identical, never invented.
  2. Townsfolk requests. Templates whose numbers are drawn from the child's own wage and costs, so "forty flyers by Friday" is always a meaningful ask and never a trivial one.
  3. The Ledger. Every objective re-asked in a new context on the retrieval schedule. Volume = objectives × contexts × the year — and it is the assessment. This is the largest single source of legitimate daily content.
  4. The world state machine. Weather, tide, season, festival, a strike at the harbour, a price rise: events that change the arithmetic of a familiar surface, so a place the child knows poses a problem they don't.
  5. Difficulty tied to the number spine. Change Rush at M3 and Change Rush at M11 are different games. Every game declares the rungs it plays at and rises with the child instead of being replaced.

The year above the day

Thirty minutes of the same five things dies in week three. The year needs structure at three scales, and only the smallest exists today. The season — twelve weeks, four a year — is what "month two" is made of: a district that opens or visibly changes, one new instrument, a goal set in week one, and a finale that resolves it.

Year one · four seasons and a review

2 · The lean weeks13–24The Old HarbourKEEP GUARDWork that varies with the tide; a bill nobody planned for; the buffer that saves you; the first scam attempt

Years two and three re-run the same six strands at higher rungs of the number spine, on the same map, with the child's own town as it now stands. The map is the constant; the maths is what rises.

Constraints that keep it alive

Testable rules, not aspirations. Each one should fail a build.

  • No two consecutive sessions present the same shape of decision. Not the same content — the same shape. Two "pick the cheaper one" days in a row is the boredom failure.
  • Every session contains one thing never seen before. Even if small.
  • Every session contains one thing exactly as it was left. Continuity is comfort. A world that is entirely new every day is not a world.
  • Nothing is available only today. Deadlines are good teaching. Disappearing rewards are engagement bait and are banned.
  • Missing a day costs nothing. No streak loss, no decay, no catch-up penalty. A child anxious about a streak is a cancelled subscription, and compulsion is not a money skill.
  • The curriculum advances on sessions; the world advances on the calendar. A child who plays three days a week gets the whole curriculum, over more weeks. Nothing is missed by not playing daily — only the town moves on without them, which is true, and is the lesson.
  • Thirty minutes is a ceiling, not a floor to farm. After about thirty-five minutes the day's paying work is genuinely done and the app says so kindly. A children's money app that is honest about stopping is the one a parent trusts — and it is a differentiator the ad-funded competition structurally cannot copy.
C

The parent report

The renewal engine

Report learning, not usage

"Ahana played 40 minutes, 5-day streak" is worthless and faintly insulting — it tells a parent what they could see from the sofa. This is worth money:

What a weekly report should sound like

Ahana can now work out which of two sizes is better value. She chose correctly seven times out of eight this week — including twice when the bigger box was the worse deal, which is the one that catches most adults.

The first is a usage metric. The second is a claim about a child, it is defensible from the mastery record, and it is the reason someone renews.

Seven items, weekly, in this order

#ItemWhy it earns its place
1One sentence at the topWhat changed this week, in human words. If nothing changed, it says so.
2What movedObjectives that reached retained or transferred, in plain English — never codes, never a percentage of a syllabus
3One decision, told as a storyWith the road not taken. The most-read item in the report — and impossible unless decisions are logged with their alternatives.
4What she found hardIncluding anything lapsed. A report that only goes up is not believed.
5The conversation cardOne question for the table, the answer the app would accept, and why. The highest-value item in the product: it makes the parent a teacher rather than an audience.
6One thing to do in real life"Let her pay at the shop and count the change back."
7What's nextSo a parent is never surprised by what their child was taught
Never in the report

A streak. A leaderboard. A comparison with other children. A percentile. A nudge to increase screen time. Any offer.

Cadence

ReportWhenPurpose
Weekly digestSundayThe habit. The renewal leading indicator.
Monthly summaryMonth endProgress across the mastery map
Season reportEvery 12 weeksThe milestone. Printable, keepable, sendable to a grandparent — the strongest referral surface in the product.

What must be logged for any of this to be writable

The report cannot be written from the data the app keeps today. Every consequential choice needs a record carrying the alternative not taken — that is what makes a sentence worth reading.

{
  "t": 1756339200000,
  "objective": "OWE-3",
  "surface": "bank",
  "chose": "wait",
  "alternatives": [{ "id": "borrow", "cost": 340, "label": "Loan for the bicycle" }],
  "reversed": false,           // did they change their mind, and how fast
  "outcome_at": 1757548800000  // when the consequence actually landed
}

Two things the app fails today

  • There is no adult gate. Any child can open the Parents page and press Jump to the next pay day and Add 200 XP. They are honestly-labelled prototype tools, but while they are reachable the economy is decorative and the app teaches that the way to get money is to cheat time — the exact failure CLAUDE.md names.
  • There is no read-only child view of their own record. A child should be able to see what they have learned. They should not be able to change it.

Staying honest about evidence

  • A baseline at onboarding and a re-measure each season, so movement is measurable.
  • Assessment separate from teaching, or the movement means nothing.
  • A season checkpoint producing one defensible statement per strand.
  • Never a claim of the form "children using this improve X%" until there is a study that says so. Nothing in the marketing may assert an outcome the mastery record cannot demonstrate for that individual child.

The report is generated from the child's own simulation state. It contains what happened in Bizzington and nothing else — it never contains, infers or asks about the family's real money, and no behavioural profile is built. That is a selling point, not just compliance. Say it plainly on the parents page.

What this means for the build

Modules · metrics · order

ModuleOwns
content/objectives.jsonThe 72-objective graph — ids, prerequisites, surfaces, parent lines. Nothing else can be built first.
content/assess/*.jsonRetrieval and transfer items, keyed to objectives
src/mastery.jsThe per-objective state machine. Single source of truth for anything reported.
src/ledger.jsThe spaced-retrieval scheduler — decides what today's lesson beat is
src/decisions.jsThe consequential-choice log
src/season.jsThe twelve-week arc state machine, world events, the weekly rhythm

src/placement.jsAdaptive placement on the number spine
—An adult gate on the Parents page, and the server clock. Small, and blocking.

Existing modules change as follows: every card in content.js gains an objective; every game declares the M-rungs it plays at; every surface that can demonstrate an objective calls into mastery.js when it does. sim.js keeps owning the money and gains nothing here.

What to measure

MetricWhy
Objectives retained per active weekThe only real product metric. Everything else is a proxy.

Order of work

  1. objectives.json for one strand — CHOOSE, twelve rungs, end to end: objective, teaching card, three assessment contexts, transfer surfaces, parent line.
  2. mastery.js and ledger.js, and instrument the shop and the market for transfer.
  3. report.js — the weekly digest, generated for that one strand.
  4. Read it as a parent. If the report is not worth reading for one strand, six strands will not fix it — the shape is wrong and must be fixed before any content volume is authored.
  5. Only then author the other five strands and the season arcs.
The bet

Steps 1–4 are perhaps a fifth of the work and settle whether the product is real. Nothing about payments, accounts or acquisition should be touched until a parent has read that one report and wanted the next one.
