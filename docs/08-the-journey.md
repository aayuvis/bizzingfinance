# 08 — The Journey: arithmetic at 8, an entrepreneur at 14

The product is not a set of features and not a corpus of artwork. It is **one journey, seven
years long**, and the asset is the simulation that makes it true.

> **You work for money. Then your money works. Then you build the thing that works
> without you.**

Three acts, seven years, one spine. Every year has a maths gate, an asset class it unlocks, a
capability you could watch a child demonstrate, and one question they can answer at the end of
it that they could not answer at the start.

---

## 1. The engine that has to exist first

Today the market is **four independent random walks with a crash hard-coded at 55% of the
series**. Four coin flips are not an economy, and nothing built on them can teach the only
lesson that matters at the top of this ladder:

> Rates went up. My bond fell, my mortgage got dearer, and my shop's loan cost more — all at
> once, for **one** reason.

So the first asset to build is **one world model** that everything else is derived from:

| Driver | Moves | Which then moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Inflation** | slow | cash's real value ↓ · shop prices ↑ · wages ↑ (later) · the goal you were saving for gets further away |
| **Policy rate** | in steps, with regimes | deposit interest ↑ · **bond prices ↓** · mortgage cost ↑ · the business loan ↑ |
| **Growth / the cycle** | boom → slowdown → contraction → recovery | company earnings · job availability on the Row · your shop's footfall |
| **A shock** | rare, not scheduled | everything at once, and it is never on the same day twice |

Three rules for the engine, and they are what make it an asset rather than a random-number
generator:

1. **One world, derived.** No asset class gets its own private randomness. Every return is a
   function of the world state plus that class's own character. This is what makes a child able
   to say *why*.
2. **Deterministic per child.** Seeded, so a season replays identically, a parent can be shown
   what happened, and a bug is reproducible.
3. **Explicitly Bizzington's own economy, never a prediction.** It is *calibrated to resemble*
   real behaviour and it says so on screen. CONCEPT §6.6 — never teach a number from memory —
   means the app must not imply that any figure here is a real-world return.

**This is the defendable asset.** Not artwork, not blurbs: an internally coherent economy that
behaves correctly, which is testable, and which a language model cannot produce in an afternoon
because it has to be *right* rather than plausible.

## 2. The asset-class ladder

Each class arrives **when the maths that makes it comprehensible arrives** — not on a birthday.
This is the spine of the whole product.

| Age | Unlocks | What it teaches | Maths it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8** | **Cash** | It is safe and it does nothing. The first asset class is the one in your hand. | M1–M3 |
| **9** | **Deposit** | Interest — the first time money moves without you working | M4–M6 |
| **10** | **Inflation** *(not a class — the thing that ends the first illusion)* | Cash was never safe. It just loses quietly. | M7–M9 |
| **11** | **Bonds** | You can be the lender. A coupon is somebody else's cost. | M10 |
| **11** | **Debt** | The same machine pointed the other way — and it has a price, never a shame | M10 |
| **12** | **Equities** | Owning a piece of something that earns | M12 |
| **12** | **Index funds** | Owning all of it, cheaply. Fees are a return you hand away. | M14, M16 |
| **12** | **Gold** | A store of value that pays nothing — and why you might still want it | M13 |
| **12** | **Property** | Leverage, rent, transaction cost, and the fact you cannot sell half a house | M13 |
| **13** | **Your own business** | The illiquid one you control, and the only one whose returns you can change | M17 |
| **14** | **Capital** | What a thing is worth, and to whom | all |

**The idea that closes the loop:** at 14, *your business is a position in your own portfolio.*
The entrepreneur endpoint is not outside the investing curriculum — it is the last asset class,
and the one where diversification finally gets uncomfortable.

## 3. The seven years

### Act I · You work for money

#### Year 1 · Age 8 — Where money comes from
- **Maths gate** M1–M3 · recognise coins, add and subtract within 100, skip-count
- **Unlocks** Cash · the wallet · jobs on Market Row
- **Can do by the end** Earns, counts change correctly, and names the thing they gave up
- **The question they can now answer** *"What did that cost you, apart from the money?"*

#### Year 2 · Age 9 — Keeping it
- **Maths gate** M4–M6 · halves and quarters, × and ÷, division with a remainder
- **Unlocks** The deposit account · jars · a named goal
- **Can do by the end** Splits income by a rule they set themselves, and reaches a goal they named
- **The question** *"How many weeks?"*

#### Year 3 · Age 10 — The plan
- **Maths gate** M7–M9 · ×12 and ×52, decimals, percent as per-hundred
- **Unlocks** A weekly budget · the buffer · **inflation**
- **Can do by the end** Runs a budget that survives a bill nobody planned for
- **The question** *"What does this cost in a year?"*
- **The turn:** this is where cash stops being the safe thing. It is the first time the app
  takes something away from a child that they thought they had.

### Act II · Your money works

#### Year 4 · Age 11 — Money that works
- **Maths gate** M10–M11 · percent *of* an amount, repeated percent
- **Unlocks** Bonds · borrowing · the rate/price relationship
- **Can do by the end** Explains compounding *in coins*, and can say what a loan costs before taking it
- **The question** *"Who is paying whom, and how much?"*

#### Year 5 · Age 12 — Risk and spread
- **Maths gate** M12–M15 · percent change, ratio, average and spread, reading a chart
- **Unlocks** Equities · index funds · gold · property. The whole board.
- **Can do by the end** Builds a mix, writes the rule down, and holds it through a bad month
- **The question** *"What happens to this in a bad year?"*
- **The big idea is correlation.** Things move together or they do not, and that — not "lots of
  different things" — is what diversification actually is. It needs the world model in §1 to be
  true at all.

### Act III · You build the thing that works

#### Year 6 · Age 13 — Making something
- **Maths gate** M16–M17 · weighted average, division back to a threshold (break-even)
- **Unlocks** The shop, run properly · unit economics · pricing
- **Can do by the end** Prices a product from its unit cost, finds break-even, and survives a
  month where profit and cash disagree
- **The question** *"How many do I have to sell?"*

#### Year 7 · Age 14 — Capital
- **Maths gate** all of it, applied together
- **Unlocks** The P&L · the balance sheet · raising money · valuation
- **Can do by the end** See §4
- **The question** *"What is this worth, and to whom?"*

## 4. What "an entrepreneur at 14" actually means

A slogan unless it is a checklist. Eight things, each observable, each assessable:

| # | Can do | Where it is demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Price a thing** from unit cost — knows COGS, margin, break-even | The shop |
| 2 | **Explain why a profitable business dies** — cash is not profit, and working capital is the reason | A month where the P&L is green and the till is empty |
| 3 | **Read a P&L and a balance sheet** and say what each is for | The books |
| 4 | **Build a twelve-week cash-flow forecast** and be wrong in a way they can explain | The planning table |
| 5 | **Choose between debt and equity** and say what each one costs — including the half you gave away | Raising for the second shop |
| 6 | **Run a business through a downturn and survive it** | A season where the cycle turns |
| 7 | **Value something roughly** and defend the number | Buying out a rival's stall |
| 8 | **Hold a diversified portfolio and defend the mix** — including the awkward fact that most of their wealth is now one illiquid business they control | The Exchange, at 14 |

**Number 8 is the one that makes the whole journey cohere.** A child who has built a business
and also learned diversification arrives at exactly the tension every real founder lives in.
That is the last lesson, and nothing else in this market teaches it.

## 5. Rules the journey imposes

- **It is a ladder, not a calendar.** A child arriving at 11 starts at their maths, not at year
  4. Entry is by placement (docs/05 §A7), never by birthday, and never by a number shown to
  the child.
- **The maths gate is real.** A surface does not open because a child is old enough. It opens
  because the arithmetic it demands has been met — docs/03 §1, already enforced in `ledger.js`.
- **Every class must be *felt* before it is named.** A child meets inflation as the goal getting
  further away, and only then learns the word.
- **Nothing is skipped and nothing is rushed.** Bizzing Bee's promise — *every word waits its
  turn* — is this app's too.
- **The end is capability, not a business.** No real money ever changes hands (CONCEPT §6.1).
  The claim is that a 14-year-old *could* run one, not that they have.
- **The age column is a design assumption, not a cited fact.** docs/03 already footnotes this,
  and it must be checked against the real curricula of the markets we ship to before it appears
  in a single marketing claim.

## 6. Where the app actually is against this

| Year | State |
|---|---|
| **1 · Age 8** | Mostly built — wallet, jobs (now games), needs vs wants |
| **2 · Age 9** | Built — jars, goals, the deposit account |
| **3 · Age 10** | Partly — budget and bills exist; **inflation does not exist at all** |
| **4 · Age 11** | Partly — the bank lends and prices it honestly; **no bonds, no rate/price link** |
| **5 · Age 12** | **Weak.** Four fictional assets on independent random walks. No correlation, no gold, no property, no index, no fees. This is the biggest gap. |
| **6 · Age 13** | Thin — a shop exists; no unit economics, no break-even, no working capital |
| **7 · Age 14** | **Does not exist.** No P&L, no balance sheet, no raising, no valuation. |

**Two of the seven years are missing and one is a random-number generator.** The journey is
roughly 40% built, and the missing 60% is the half a parent would pay for — because it is the
half that ends with a fourteen-year-old who can run something.

---

## 7. Order of work

1. **The world model** (§1). Inflation, policy rate, cycle. Everything else derives from it.
   Nothing above year 3 is honest until this exists.
2. **Rebuild the asset classes on it** — bonds first, because the rate/price link is the
   clearest proof the engine works and it is the thing four random walks can never show.
3. **Add the missing classes** — index (with a fee drag), gold, property (with leverage).
4. **Year 6** — unit economics, break-even, and a month where profit and cash disagree.
5. **Year 7** — the P&L, the balance sheet, raising, valuation.
6. **Assert the whole thing.** A test that runs a simulated child through seven years and
   checks the world stayed coherent: rates up and bonds fell; cash lost to inflation over a
   decade; the diversified player beat the concentrated one on risk-adjusted terms across a
   full cycle; the boring player won the season (CONCEPT §6.3).

Step 6 is what turns this from a plan into an asset. **A simulation nobody has tested across
seven years is a claim; one with the assertions above is a product.**
