The Third Sibling

Bizzing Finance measured against Bizzing Bee and Bizzing India on the family's own yardstick — the one India wrote in its docs/10 and Finance borrowed in its docs/06 — and eight moves that would make it an equal, ranked by what each one actually produces.

The gap is not a features gap. Bee and India each answer seven questions a parent can repeat to another parent; Finance answers three. Two of the four it cannot answer — the corpus and the deadline — are the two that make a family renew for three years.

3 · The edge

What only Finance has, and should build on

A strategy that only copies the siblings would make Finance a third content app with a smaller corpus. Two things are structurally Finance's alone, and every move below is chosen so that it borrows a sibling's form while standing on one of these.

It is a simulation, not a library

Bee and India ship authored corpora; their content is finite because somebody wrote it. Finance has a world model, seven asset classes priced off it, a business with a balance sheet that balances to 10⁻¹¹ over 300 weeks, and a forty-year market. It can generate situations forever — the one thing a library cannot do. The atom Finance never named is sitting here: a situation the child had to decide in.

The subject has a clock built in

India had to argue its deadline ("grandparents do not wait"). Finance's is arithmetic: the app already teaches it in c7a. A child who starts at eight compounds six more years than one who starts at fourteen, and the difference is not small. Nobody has said this to a parent yet. It is the only deadline in the family that gets more true every year.

4 · Eight moves

Ranked by what each one produces

Every move names the sibling precedent it borrows, the asset it leaves behind, and an honest effort. Policy choices — anything a competitor changes in a sprint — are excluded on purpose; that list was written once already and called what it was.

1

A real bee for money

the deadline

Bee's whole engine works because the bee is a real event with a date and a published word list. Money has one too: NCFE's annual National Financial Literacy Assessment Test for classes VI–XII in India, and the Council for Economic Education's National Personal Finance Challenge in the US. Map the curriculum to the published syllabus, and the app gains a calendar, a canonical list, and a reason a parent signs up in September.

2

Narrate the town

the pipeline exists

India's 11,506 clips are what make it feel finished; Finance has 165. The pipeline built this week records, measures and rejects a silent clip automatically, so the marginal clip costs minutes. Every letter and each of its outcomes, every shopkeeper's line, the market-day report, the shock when the monsoon comes — all of it spoken, in the neutral voice.

BorrowsIndia — every scene narrated; audio by keyProduces~3,000 clips; the app's second-biggest asset after the enginesEffort2 weeks, mostly waiting for the synthesiser
3

The Register

the corpus

The atom a child already loves: who makes this? Five hundred real companies — what they make, who pays them, what could hurt them — collected the way India lights up states: "you can name the maker of 84 things in your house." Read-only, behind the hard wall in CONCEPT §6.2; the Exchange keeps its fictional names and its buy button. The asset is not the blurbs, which a model writes in an afternoon, but the verification trail and the house voice.

BorrowsBee — a canonical, finite list, nothing skippedProducesA sourced, countable corpus with a completion meterEffort8 weeks for the first 50, deep not wide — docs/06 §6
4

The Almanac

history, sourced

Forty narrated episodes of market history — 1929, 1987, 2000, 2008, 2020, India's 1991 — told exactly as India tells Itihaas: badged, with sources[] on every claim, a human reviewer on anything contested. The Market Game already replays the behaviour of these years under fictional names; the Almanac gives them their dates and their faces.

BorrowsIndia — the editorial policy and its headless gateProducesA second corpus, dramatic, finite, narratableEffort6 weeks for the first six episodes
5

The heirloom ledger

the completion meter

Bee's meter is a percentage; India's is a map. Finance's should be the child's own net-worth line from eight to fourteen, with every decision underneath it — decisions.js already keeps them. At fourteen the family gets it printed and bound: six years in Bizzington, the crossover chart where the shop first out-earned the shifts, the goals that went up floor by floor. The family already prints books.

BorrowsIndia — the map as the share artefact; bizzing-bee-booksProducesA longitudinal record: worthless on day one, a switching cost by year twoEffort6 weeks for the meter; the book at year two
6

The parent's own gap

the promise

India's sharpest insight: the parent absorbed their culture; the child has to be taught it, and the parent never learned how. Money is the same shape, harder — most parents were never taught it at all. The promise is one sentence: "She'll know at twelve what you learnt at thirty-five." And the method India hands the parent becomes, here, the Sunday three — three dinner-table questions drawn from what the child actually did this week. Never about the household's own money; that rule stands.

BorrowsIndia docs/11 — "hand the parent a method"ProducesThe promise, and a parent report that becomes a conversationEffort2 weeks; the report already exists
7

Sunday in Bizzington

someone in the room

India's inventory ends with two things an app cannot give — fifteen cousins, being in the majority — "but belonging can be simulated." Finance's version is pass-and-play: the board game and a market season played by a whole family on one screen, the parent given a fictional household of their own to run against the child's. The one hook in docs/07 that is weekend-shaped, and the only one where an adult learns something too.

BorrowsIndia — belonging simulated; docs/07 H7ProducesA weekly ritual; a second adult who renewsEffort3 weeks; the board game engine exists
8

Outcome evidence

slow · strongest

A pre/post study: the mastery record against an independent instrument, a real cohort, a real gap. Bee tests learning outcomes better than India does; nobody in children's finance has published any. It is expensive, slow and unfakeable — which is exactly why it would be the only moat in the category that a competitor cannot rent, generate or copy in a sprint.

5 · The ninety days

In order, because the order matters

  1. Weeks 1–2
    Narrate the town, and say the promise

    Moves 2 and 6. The cheapest two, and the two that change how the app feels the day they land. Every letter spoken; the parent report ending with the Sunday three; the promise on the first screen a parent sees.

  2. Weeks 3–6
    Map to the bee, register the first fifty

    Moves 1 and 3, together, because the syllabus decides which fifty companies come first. This is the month the corpus and the deadline both appear, and the parent gets a number to repeat.

  3. Weeks 7–12
    The Almanac's first six, the meter's first line

    Moves 4 and 5. Six narrated episodes under the editorial gate; the net-worth line drawn from day one so that year two has something to print.

  4. Week 12 →
    Design the study, run it on the bee cohort

    Move 8 starts on paper now and in classrooms when move 1's first cohort sits the test. Move 7, the Sunday game, is the reward for shipping the rest — weekend-shaped work for a weekend.
