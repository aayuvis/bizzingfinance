The idea that closes the loop

At 14, the child's business is a position in their own portfolio. The entrepreneur endpoint is not outside the investing curriculum — it is the last asset class, and the one where diversification finally gets uncomfortable.

The eight capabilities below

“What is this worth, and to whom?”

What “an entrepreneur at 14” actually means

A slogan unless it is a checklist — eight observable, assessable things

  1. Price a thing from unit costKnows COGS, margin, break-even · demonstrated in the shop
  2. Explain why a profitable business diesCash is not profit, and working capital is the reason · a month where the P&L is green and the till is empty
  3. Read a P&L and a balance sheetAnd say what each one is for
  4. Build a twelve-week cash-flow forecastAnd be wrong in a way they can explain
  5. Choose between debt and equityAnd say what each costs — including the half you gave away
  6. Run a business through a downturn and survive itA season where the cycle turns
  7. Value something roughly and defend the numberBuying out a rival's stall
  8. Hold a diversified portfolio and defend the mixIncluding the awkward fact that most of their wealth is now one illiquid business they control. This is the one that makes the whole journey cohere — a child who has built a business and also learned diversification arrives at exactly the tension every real founder lives in.

Where the app actually is

Two of the seven years do not exist and one is a random-number generator

YearStateWhat is there
1 · 8mostly builtWallet, jobs (now games), needs vs wants
2 · 9builtJars, goals, the deposit account
3 · 10partlyBudget and bills exist. Inflation does not exist at all.
4 · 11partlyThe bank lends and prices it honestly. No bonds, no rate/price link.
5 · 12weakFour fictional assets on independent random walks. No correlation, no gold, no property, no index, no fees. The biggest gap.
6 · 13thinA shop exists. No unit economics, no break-even, no working capital.
7 · 14absentNo P&L, no balance sheet, no raising, no valuation.

The journey is roughly 40% built, and the missing 60% is the half a parent would pay for — because it is the half that ends with a fourteen-year-old who can run something.

Order of work

Nothing above year 3 is honest until step one exists

  1. The world modelInflation, policy rate, cycle. Everything else derives from it.
  2. Rebuild the asset classes on it — bonds firstThe rate/price link is the clearest proof the engine works, and the thing four random walks can never show
  3. Add the missing classesIndex with a fee drag, gold, property with leverage
  4. Year 6Unit economics, break-even, and a month where profit and cash disagree
  5. Year 7The P&L, the balance sheet, raising, valuation
  6. Assert the whole thingRun a simulated child through seven years and check the world stayed coherent: rates up and bonds fell; cash lost to inflation over a decade; the diversified player beat the concentrated one on risk-adjusted terms across a full cycle; the boring player won the season.
What turns the plan into an asset

A simulation nobody has tested across seven years is a claim. One with those assertions is a product.
