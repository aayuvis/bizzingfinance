# 04 — Why it's boring, and what actually fixes it

Bizzington has a simulator, a curriculum, five worlds, eleven games and three quests a day.
It is still boring. More systems will not fix that, because the problem is not a missing
feature — it is that **three engines every child's game runs on are absent.**

---

## 1. The honest diagnosis

### Nothing is at stake
You cannot lose. Rent goes out on Friday and if you cannot pay it, *nothing happens.* A
budget with no consequence is a spreadsheet. Every game a child plays for half an hour has
jeopardy in it somewhere, and this has none.

### Nothing visibly changes
Money goes up. A level ticks. The street looks identical on day forty to how it looked on
day one. Compare what actually holds children for months — a town that gets rebuilt, an
island that fills up, a farm that grows. **Progress you can see beats progress you can
count**, and we shipped only the countable kind.

### Nobody wants anything from you
Pip says lines. Nana says wise things. Mags sells. But **no character ever needs something**,
so there is no reason to care what any of them think, and no relationship that changes.

### The reward is a number, and numbers are not rewards
You earn money and the money sits there. The Store sells hats. A hat does nothing. So the
loop terminates in an abstraction, and an eight-year-old correctly concludes that nothing
they did mattered.

### The half hour has no shape
There is no session arc — no opening, no build, no pay-off, no hook for tomorrow. Thirty
minutes has to be *designed*, not hoped for.

---

## 2. The fix, in the order it matters

### A. The town is broken, and you rebuild it

This is the big one, and it turns every system already built into visible progress.

Bizzington is **run down**. The fountain is dry, the bakery is shuttered, the harbour crane
is seized, the clocktower has stopped. Each world carries four or five **restorations** —
things you can pay to put right.

Fixing one:
- **costs real money out of your wallet**, competing directly with the Store and your goals,
- **permanently changes the art of that world**, and
- **gives a permanent capability** — the bakery reopening adds a job that pays every day, the
  fountain earns the town an extra quest slot, the crane raises what harbour work pays.

That is the loop the app is missing, and it carries the strongest money lesson in the whole
product: **money spent on something that produces is not the same as money spent on
something that doesn't.** A child discovers that by watching one choice pay them back every
day afterwards and the other one not.

### B. The Store sells capability, not hats

Every item does something:

| | Does |
|---|---|
| **Handcart** | a third job each day |
| **Lockbox** | the Save jar earns a little every pay day, before the bank exists |
| **Bicycle** | reach the next world's work a world early |
| **Ledger** | see next week's bills *before* pay day lands |
| **Good coat** | bad-weather work pays more |
| **Shop cat** | finds a little, some mornings, for no reason |

Two purely lovely things stay, priced beside the useful ones with the opportunity-cost line
under both — because the choice between *useful* and *lovely* is a real one and the app
should not pretend otherwise.

### C. A week can go wrong

If wallet and Spend jar together cannot cover Friday, you are **in arrears**. Nana covers it
the first time and says so plainly. Miss twice and you lose the room and move *down* the
housing ladder — recoverable, never permanent, never framed as a failure of character.

Jeopardy is what makes the budget matter. Without it the Jar Shed is a toy.

### D. The thirty minutes, designed

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **0–1 min** | The letter | one decision, always a small surprise |
| **1–4 min** | The day's work | jobs, and what the town needs today |
| **4–16 min** | Two quests | one lesson, one game — the quests are drawn to force the mix |
| **16–26 min** | The long one | Main Street, or a restoration you've been saving for |
| **26–29 min** | The town changes | the thing you paid for is *there* now, and Pip says so |
| **29–30 min** | Tomorrow's hook | "the baker needs 200 by Friday" — stated before you close |

The last row is the one that brings a child back, and it is currently missing entirely.

### E. Somebody wants something

Townsfolk carry **requests** with deadlines: *"Forty flyers by Friday — sixty for the job."*
Multi-day, refusable, and they remember. Do enough for someone and they open something: a
better rate, a shortcut, a job nobody else gets offered.

---

## 3. The five worlds, concretely

Each world is **a place with a problem**. You arrive, the problem is visible, and the work
you do fixes it — which is what turns earning into progress instead of arithmetic.

---

### 1 · 🧺 Market Row — *you start here*

**You start with a room above a stall and 120 in your pocket. Rent is due Friday.**
That is the whole opening: no tutorial, no gift, a bill with a date on it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What's wrong** | The Row is half-empty. The fountain is dry, half the stalls have lost their awnings, and Nana's shop at the end has been shuttered since she retired. |
| **Who's here** | Pip on the next stall · Nana, retired · Mrs Rao, who always needs flyers · the grain seller, who has crates and no time |
| **Work going** | Stack crates · deliver flyers · sweep the Row |
| **Requests** | *"Forty flyers by Friday — sixty for the job."* Multi-day, refusable, remembered. |
| **You rebuild** | The dry fountain **120** → the town can carry a fourth daily quest · flower boxes **60** → streak pays more · awnings **80** → work pays more in the rain · **Nana's shutters 200** → a new job every day, forever |
| **You learn** | What money even is · Earning it |
| **This opens** | Your wallet, and the room above the stall |
| **Capstone** | Four weeks' rent, none of them missed |

---

### 2 · ⚓ The Old Harbour

| | |
|---|---|
| **What's wrong** | The crane is seized and the tide board is blank, so boats land and nobody is ready for them. |
| **Who's here** | The harbour master, gruff, keeps the only tide chart in his head · a skipper permanently in a hurry · Mags, mending umbrellas |
| **Work going** | Mend the nets · unload cargo · mend umbrellas |
| **The hook** | **Cargo pays double if you are already at the quay when the boat lands** — and you can only know when that is if you planned. The Harbour teaches planning by paying for it. |
| **You rebuild** | The crane **180** → cargo pays more · the tide board **120** → *see when boats land* · the net loft **90** → nets every day · the pier lamp **60** → night work |
| **You learn** | Making a plan · Sellers and their tricks |
| **This opens** | The Jar Shed and the Build Yard |
| **Capstone** | One whole week inside a plan you set yourself |

---

### 3 · 🕰️ Clocktower Square

| | |
|---|---|
| **What's wrong** | **The clock has stopped**, so nobody knows when anything falls due. Bills arrive late and cost more for it. |
| **Who's here** | A precise, anxious bank clerk · a pawnbroker — Mags's cousin — who offers fast money at a price · Nana, visiting |
| **Work going** | Run the errands · do Nana's books |
| **The hook** | While the clock is stopped **you cannot see what is due next**. Restoring it is what makes the square legible — a mechanic whose whole content is *know the date the money leaves*. |
| **You rebuild** | The clock **250** → see every bill before it lands · the bank steps **100** → a better rate · the notice board **80** → town requests appear · the basin **140** → more interest |
| **You learn** | Keeping it safe · Borrowing |
| **This opens** | The Bank, then borrowing |
| **Capstone** | Take a loan, work out the total cost before the app tells you, clear it |

### 4 · 📈 The Exchange Quarter

| | |
|---|---|
| **What's wrong** | The board is chalked wrong and Bo and Bea have been arguing so long that nobody is trading. |
| **Who's here** | Bo · Bea · a floor manager · **Boring Bella, in person** — not just a bot in a game |
| **Work going** | Run the orders · chalk up the board |
| **The hook** | You can **see other people's holdings and what became of them.** Bella's is dull and up. Chaser's is thrilling and flat. Nobody has to say the lesson. |
| **You rebuild** | The ticker **300** → live prices · the reading room **200** → research cards · Bella's bench **150** → she will actually teach you |
| **You learn** | Money that grows |
| **This opens** | The Exchange |
| **Capstone** | Write a one-sentence plan and hold it through a red week |

### 5 · 🏭 The Works

| | |
|---|---|
| **What's wrong** | The workshops are cold. Nothing is being made. |
| **Who's here** | Nana, unretired and advising · a supplier who wants paying before you have sold anything |
| **Work going** | The shop itself |
| **The hook** | **Cash flow.** The supplier wants paying Tuesday and the customers come Saturday. |

## 4. The quests they take

Three a day, drawn so the mix is forced — never three of the same kind:

| Kind | Example | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Learn** | Finish a card | small |
| **Work** | Take two jobs on the Row | small |
| **Play** | Play two games · finish a Main Street | medium |
| **Decide** | Put 20 away · feed the Build Yard · spot a scam | medium |
| **Town** | Put 40 towards the fountain | medium |
| **Request** | *Mrs Rao's forty flyers, by Friday* — runs several days | large |

Clear all three and there is a bonus. A **weekly** sits above them: one bigger commitment
with a deadline, paying much more than a day's work, which is the first time a child has to
plan across days rather than inside one.

## 5. What this is not

- **Not a second currency.** Restorations and the Store are paid for out of the same wallet
  as everything else (CONCEPT §3.1).
- **Not loot.** Nothing is randomised-for-payment, ever (CONCEPT §6.3). The cat finding a coin
  is not a reward for spending; it is a fixed effect of a thing you chose to buy.
- **Not a fail state that shames.** Arrears is a thing that happens to careful people, and the
  app says so in Nana's voice, exactly as the month that will not balance does (docs/03 §7).
