Bizzing Finance · Engagement

Three Visits,
Not One Hour

An hour a day for 365 days is 365 hours — more than almost any children's app ever gets. This one has banned every cheap way of getting it. Here is what would earn it honestly, and what I actually think is reachable.

An hour a day is not one hour. It is three visits.

Morning: what happened overnight. After school: the work and the build. Evening: something with someone else in the room. Design for visits, not sessions — a single sixty-minute block is a thing a child does twice and then never again.

Nine USPs

Not features — things a competitor cannot copy without becoming a different product

#USPWhy nobody else has it
1One currency, and it is the curriculumEvery competitor has gems and coins. Here the money you earn playing is the money you spend, so the trade-off is the lesson. A second currency teaches that magic money arrives for showing up.
2The simulator is closed. No real money, everNo card, no bank link, no cash-out. That is why it needs no financial licence in any market — and why a parent can hand over the phone without reading the terms.
3Scored on the decision, never the returnA game scored on returns has taught gambling. Here the boring diversified player wins the season — a thing an ad-funded competitor structurally cannot ship.
4Honest about stoppingThe only money app for children that says the day's work is done. Unfakeable by anyone whose revenue is attention.
5500 real companiesThe corpus, and the moat. Nobody hands a child the actual register with a ladder through it.
6Reports learning, not usage“Ahana can compare unit prices — 7 of 8 this week, twice when the big box was worse”, not “40 minutes, 5-day streak”.
7A town that permanently carries her nameRestorations are irreversible and the deed is public. Progress you point at, not a number.
8No ads, no tracking, minimal data by constructionFirst name and an age band. DPDP/COPPA-native rather than retrofitted.
9Family-shaped, not stranger-shapedThe social layer is the household. No open chat, no global leaderboard, nobody to compare a child unfavourably with.

The seven hooks

Each rated by minutes it can honestly hold · red = not built

H1

The overnight

The oldest hook in tycoon games, and here it is not a trick because it is true: interest accrued, the shop sold, the market moved, the tide came in. The child returns to news, not a menu.

Worth 5–8 min, first visit · Lasts for ever
Status partly built — the shop and market don't run overnight yet
H2

Market rhythm

The world has a clock and it isn't the child's. Markets open and close, the board is chalked, Friday is pay day, Sunday is the report. Anticipation without a single notification.

Worth the reason a morning visit exists · Lasts for ever
Status pay day only
H3 · missing

The collection

“84 of 500 met.” Bizzing Bee's packs applied to the Register. Finite, countable, canonical, years long — with sub-collections so it never feels infinite: a completed sector, the ten oldest, the ones that make things in your own kitchen.

Worth 10 min a visit, endlessly · Lasts 2–3 years
Status not built — the biggest missing engine
H4

Mastery

Personal bests per job, difficulty that climbs with the ladder, your own past run as the ghost you race. Never another child's score — the competitor is you last week.

Worth 10–15 min a visit · Lasts while the ceiling moves
Status built for jobs; no ghosts yet
H5 · missing

Ownership and expression

The engine the app lacks entirely. Children give hours to Minecraft and Roblox because they build. Bizzington has a town they repair but do not shape — no placing, no naming, no choosing a sign, no laying out a shop floor.

Worth 15–20 min, the strongest weekend hook there is
Status not built
H6

Consequence with a delay

A decision that resolves later is remembered; one that resolves instantly is a button. The loan that costs you every pay day. The stock you panicked out of. The buffer that saved you.

Worth not minutes — the reason the minutes matter
Status the loan and the market do this; little else does
H7 · missing

Someone else in the room

The evening visit needs a second person. Pass-and-play Main Street. A household net-worth board. A parent who can be beaten. A sibling's town you can visit and admire but not touch.

Worth 15–25 min — the highest-retention block in the day
Status Main Street exists but is single-player against bots

Six engines, in order of value per week of work

What to build, and why in this order

#EngineWhy here
1The Register + collection meter H3The corpus. Nothing else compounds like it — and step one needs no backend at all: fifty companies, deep.
2Build mode H5The missing engine. Place buildings, name the shop, choose the sign. Kids give hours to building and none to browsing.
3The overnight H1 H2Make the shop, bank and market actually run while away, then open with the news. Cheap to build, immediate effect on the morning visit.
4Pass-and-play + household board H7Main Street already exists; seating 2–4 humans is small work for the highest-retention block in the day.
5Commissions H6Multi-day requests with deadlines, from named people who remember.
6The Almanac H3 H6Forty canonical market episodes, playable.

The 365-day payoff

On the anniversary the app prints the child's year: what they earned, what they built, the town as it stands with their name on it, the companies they met, what they learned and held. Printable, keepable, and the thing a grandparent gets sent. Nothing else in the design makes a year feel like a thing that happened.

What we never do — and it is the USP

Eight competitor tools, all refused

  • No streak that punishes. Missing a day costs nothing. A child anxious about a streak is a cancelled subscription, and compulsion is not a money skill.
  • No loot boxes, paid spins, or randomised reward for money spent.
  • No second currency. One wallet.
  • No notifications to a child. The parent may opt into a weekly digest. The child is never pinged.
  • No global leaderboard, no comparison with other children, no percentile.
  • No limited-time anything. Deadlines are good teaching; disappearing rewards are bait.
  • No ads. No behavioural tracking. Ever.
  • No live twitching ticker that rewards reacting — the app spends a whole strand teaching that urgency is manufactured by sellers.

Every one of these is a competitor's cheapest engagement tool. Refusing all eight is precisely why a parent would pay — and the reason the hour has to be earned by the world being worth an hour.

The honest answer

Sixty minutes a day, every day, for 365 days is above what this category achieves, and I would not plan the business on it. The hour is reachable on the days that matter — weekends, season finales, the week a child first opens their shop. Chasing it on a wet Tuesday in February would mean building exactly the eight things above that we have banned.

A child who gives this thirty honest minutes on a school day and two hours on a Saturday is a child who has learned to run money — and a parent who renews.

The one number to hold the design to is not minutes. It is objectives retained per active week. An hour a day that teaches nothing is a failure with good telemetry.
