Six Years in Bizzington

Ahana is eight when she opens the app and is handed a stall on Market Row. She is fourteen when the app prints her a book. This is what happens in between — day by day, season by season, life by life — with every fork she can take drawn, and every feature marked as built, half-built, or proposed.

The ladder already exists: thirty levels, five ranks. What a child feels is not a ladder. She feels four lives in one town, each with a new place, a new mentor, new things she is allowed to do, one real choice that changes her street, and something at the end she can put on the fridge.

The map

Four lives, five forks, four things to keep

One day · fifteen minutes

The loop she runs on day 30, and on day 1,030

A day is the same shape for six years; what changes is what each beat contains. Nothing here needs a notification — the reasons to come back are that things happened without her, and that yesterday's choice has a consequence on a timer.

One year · four seasons

Each season ends with something to show

The daily loop holds a child for weeks. Seasons hold her for years. Each is roughly twelve weeks of the sim clock, themed to one journey, and ends in a finale — the thing a parent gets to see. This is the calendar the app does not have, and every piece of it reuses engines that already run. ○ proposed

Season of shocks

Monsoon

The Household's season. Letters arrive faster: the tap, the rent, the umbrella fund. Everyone on the street chooses. Then the sky opens.

Finale — the rain hits the whole street at once; who joined the fund and who chanced it is visible on every roof. Insurance, taught by weather.
Season of spending

Festival

The Household and the Give jar. Prices rise, the market booms, the shop's best fortnight of the year — and the temptation to spend the Save jar on lights.

Finale — the town's festival is funded from what the children gave; the restorations they paid for are lit that night, deeds and names on them.
Season of selling

The Fair

The Livelihood's season. Every stall competes for a fortnight. Rivals cut prices. Stock runs out or goes home unsold.

Finale — the Fair is scored on margin and on decisions, never on takings alone (rule 3). The boring, well-priced stall wins more often than the flashy one.
Season of the long game

The Bee

The Portfolio's season. The Market Game campaign runs a decade; the real-world test (NFLAT, or its equivalent) sits at the end of it, with a date.

Finale — the campaign's review: what she held through, what she panic-sold, what she wrote before buying. And the test result, the only number a school recognises.
The four lives

What she does, what she can newly do, what changes on her street

A life ends when its rank is reached. Its verbs are the things the town lets her do that it would not before — that is what mastery unlocks, not a number. Each life has one fork, and the fork is a real choice with two right answers that teach different things.

No fork — on purpose

An eight-year-old needs a straight road with one rule of her own on it. The jar split is her choice, and the goal is hers. The first real fork waits for the bank.

Fork A · Home — rent, or buy

The housing ladder already has five rungs and the last one is "a little house, bought" with a mortgage the sim can service. Make it a choice at level 12: keep renting (cheap, flexible, the difference goes to the bank) or take the mortgage (equity, a fixed cost that does not care about a quiet week). Both are right. One teaches flexibility, the other teaches ownership, and the Independence meter moves differently under each. ◐ the mortgage exists; the choice is not presented as one

Fork B · Borrow — the tool, or wait

A letter offers a trolley that doubles what crate-stacking pays. Borrow for it now (an asset that earns, with rent on the money) or save six more weeks. The trust score remembers either way. ○

Fork C · Temperament — saver, investor, owner

Where does the surplus live? The Saver's desk (deposits and bonds, slow and safe), the Investor's desk (the index and shares), or the Owner's desk (into her own stall). She picks one to start; the world model makes them correlate, so a bad year teaches all three at once. Bo argues for one, Bea for another, and neither of them knows. ◐ all three exist as asset classes; nothing frames them as a temperament she chooses

Fork D · The stall — maker, trader, service

Three kinds of stall on the same business engine, each teaching a different economics. Maker (bake, craft, mend): unit cost, margin, waste. Trader (buy in the Harbour, sell on Market Row): spread, inventory, the stock that goes home unsold. Service (deliveries, tutoring): pricing an hour, and the day when there are no more hours. Switching later is allowed and costs something — which is its own lesson. ○ the engine is built; the types are not

3
Her own Annual Report

In exactly the format the Market Game's companies get — revenue, cost, profit, net worth, a balance sheet that balances — with a letter to shareholders that she writes. The parent is the shareholder. Ahana's stall made a margin of 19% in its second year, up from 12; she held through a 20% fall and wrote why. ○ the company report exists; hers does not

The forks, drawn

Two right answers, different lessons

What keeps her

Eight reasons to come back, none of them a trick

CONCEPT §6.3 and the editorial policy ban the cheap ways — loot, streak pressure, countdowns, notifications to a child. What is left is better, and every mechanic here names the rule it must not break.

Things happen without her ○

The shop sells overnight, interest lands, the market moves, a letter arrives. The morning glance exists to find out. This is the single strongest reason to open an app, and the engines already produce it — nothing yet shows it.

must not become: a notification. She finds it; it does not find her.

The town changes because of her ✓

House tiers, the shop opening, restorations with her name on a deed. Progress she can walk past, not a bar she reads.

must not become: cosmetics for sale. Nothing on the street is bought with real money.

Consequences on a timer ✓

The tap she did not fix lands in four days. Anticipation without a reminder — the fuse is the app's honest version of suspense.

must not become: random. A certain flood is a lesson; a rolled one is a slot machine.

Seasons with finales ○

Four a year, each with a day the whole street shares and an artefact for the fridge. A finale is the reason a parent asks "how did it go?"

must not become: FOMO. A missed finale is replayed, never lost.

A count of what is left ◐

Worlds lit (5), restorations mended (17), badges (38) — and, once the Register exists, companies she can name (500). Bee's "Library explored" and India's map, for money.

must not become: a grind. Every item on the count teaches; none is filler.

Someone in the room ◐

Sunday in Bizzington: the board game pass-and-play, a parent with a fictional household of their own. And the Sibling Exchange — two children in one household lend to each other at interest and sell each other outgrown bikes.

must not become: a leaderboard. Rivalry between siblings is on decisions, never on money.

Mastery opens verbs ✓

Not XP — a new thing the town lets her do. Level 6 is not a number; it is the day the Jar Shed's door opens.

must not become: a paywall on the child's screen. Premium is a parent's decision elsewhere.

The heirloom ○

Six years accumulating into a printed object. Worthless on day one, and by year two the one thing that makes leaving unthinkable — not because it locks her in, but because it is hers.

must not become: data hostage. Export and delete of the whole record, always, one tap.
Build order

To make the journey real, in the order the child meets it

  1. first
    Overnight, and the first receipt

    The morning beat that shows what happened while she was away, and the purchase that becomes an object in her Collection. Both are surfacing work — the engines already produce the facts. This is Life 1 becoming a life.

  2. second
    The seasons, starting with the Monsoon

    A calendar on the sim clock, one season themed to each journey, and the Monsoon's street-wide finale built on the umbrella fund and fuses that already run. Life 2's ending.

  3. third
    Forks A and B, staged as letters

    The mortgage and the trolley loan, presented as choices with two right answers, logged with their alternatives. The housing ladder and the loan engine exist; this is the staging.

  4. fourth
    Stall types, rivals, and her Annual Report

    Maker, trader, service on the one business engine; a rival whose prices react; the Fair as the season finale; and the report in the companies' own format with her letter to the shareholder. Life 3, whole.

  5. fifth
    The crossover chart, the fast-forward, the book

    Draw the net-worth line from day one so year two has something to print; the week the shop out-earned the shifts; thirty years of compounding on one screen. Life 4's ending, and the promise kept.
